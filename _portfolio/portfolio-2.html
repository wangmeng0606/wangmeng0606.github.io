--- title: "Synthetic zwitterionic copolymer coatings" excerpt: "Connecting zwitterionic copolymer chemistry, surface energy, and hydration to scaling-resistant performance across different water chemistries." collection: portfolio thrust: "Interfacial Chemistry" thrust_order: 2 order: 3 # area: "Polymer coatings · Membrane materials" header: teaser: "ZAC.png" related_publications: - /publication/2022-09-29-Calcium Sulfate Formation on Different Zwitterionic Amphiphilic Copolymer Substrates for Salt Water Treatment - /publication/2024-09-13-Influence of zwitterionic amphiphilic copolymers on heterogeneous gypsum formation a promising approach for scaling resistance - /publication/2022-04-08-Zwitterionic polymers in biofouling and inorganic fouling mechanisms - /publication/2025-05-06-Organic fouling on zwitterionic amphiphilic copolymers implications in biofouling - /publication/2026-01-03-Zwitterion Loading Tunes Heterogeneous Gypsum Nucleation on Amphiphilic Copolymer Coatings --- Zwitterionic polymers combine positive and negative charge groups within an overall neutral material, giving them strong hydration and antifouling properties. My work investigates how these zwitterionic amphiphilic copolymers (ZACs)—which form chemically heterogeneous, self-assembled surfaces—influence calcium sulfate (gypsum) nucleation and growth, and how the same coatings resist biofouling, rather than treating hydrophilicity as the only design variable. Testing several ZAC coatings in simulated brackish water across different saturation indices showed that scaling resistance depends on different surface properties depending on water chemistry: surface energy dominates at high saturation indices, while hydrophilicity dominates at lower ones. These findings show that coating selection should account for feed-water chemistry rather than relying on a single universal surface property.
