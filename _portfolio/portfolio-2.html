--- title: "Design surface coatings with synthetic zwitterionic copolymer materials" excerpt: "Connecting zwitterionic copolymer chemistry, surface energy, and hydration to scaling-resistant performance across different water chemistries." collection: portfolio order: 2 area: "Polymer coatings · Membrane materials" header: teaser: "ZAC.png" --- Hydrophilic and hydrophobic coatings can change the energy barrier for mineral nucleation and deposition. Surface functional groups, hydration, surface energy, and ion adsorption may all influence scaling, but their relative importance can shift with the surrounding water chemistry. Zwitterionic polymers combine positive and negative groups within an overall neutral material, giving them strong hydration and antifouling properties. Zwitterionic amphiphilic copolymers (ZACs) also form chemically heterogeneous, self-assembled surfaces that may influence how calcium sulfate crystals form. My work investigates these mechanisms directly rather than treating hydrophilicity as the only design variable.

We synthesized four ZAC coatings from different combinations of zwitterionic and hydrophobic monomers, then tested their calcium sulfate scaling behavior in simulated brackish water at different saturation indices. At high saturation indices, scaling depended primarily on surface energy, and the lowest-energy PT:SBMA coating showed the strongest resistance. At lower saturation indices, hydrophilicity became the dominant factor; highly hydrated MPC-containing coatings appeared to slow scale-forming ion adsorption near the surface. The findings show that coating selection should account for feed-water chemistry instead of relying on a single universal surface property.
