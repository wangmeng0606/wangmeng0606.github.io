Predicting how temperature and pressure evolve along a wellbore is central to designing CO2-based drilling and injection operations, since CO2's density and viscosity are highly sensitive to both. I developed coupled wellbore flow and heat-transfer models—one for pure CO2 injection spanning geological sequestration, enhanced oil recovery, and fracturing, and another specifically for ultra-short radius radial well (URRW) drilling with supercritical CO2 (SC-CO2).

For URRW drilling, the model showed that CO2 reaches its supercritical state at a relatively shallow depth, and that temperature and pressure distributions differ substantially from conventional drilling because of the drillstring's unusual structure and narrow radial laterals. These models provide a basis for predicting wellbore conditions and informing equipment and operating-parameter design in CO2-based well operations.
