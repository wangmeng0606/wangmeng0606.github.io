Ion pairing and hydration strongly influence electrolyte non-ideality, ion transport, and separation in concentrated aqueous systems, yet these molecular interactions are often treated implicitly in conventional thermodynamic models. I use Raman and dielectric spectroscopy to resolve ion pairing and hydration structure in sulfate electrolytes spanning monovalent to trivalent cations, then incorporate those ion-pairing effects into an extended Pitzer model for activity coefficients—connecting molecular-scale speciation directly to a predictive thermodynamic framework for ion transport in engineered water systems.
