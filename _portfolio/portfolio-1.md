Mineral scaling remains a major challenge in reverse osmosis because it reduces permeability, lowers solute rejection, and can irreversibly damage membranes. My work examines how membrane surface chemistry changes not only how much mineral deposits, but also the structure, orientation, and coverage of the crystals that form.

Gypsum scaling tests on bare and bovine serum albumin (BSA)-conditioned membranes showed that BSA-conditioned surfaces required longer to reach the same permeability loss—even though they accumulated roughly twice the mineral mass. The BSA layer stabilized bassanite and disrupted the oriented formation of needle-like gypsum crystals, showing that controlling mineral structure and orientation at an interface can matter as much as reducing total deposition.
