Combining an antiscaling coating with an antimicrobial one can address mineral scaling and biofouling at the same time, but the two properties don't always reinforce each other. I coated a commercial reverse osmosis membrane with polyacrylic acid (PAA), an antiscaling material, and graphene oxide (GO), an antimicrobial material, alone and in combination, and tested each for antiscaling and antibiofouling performance.

The combined GO-and-PAA coating performed best, improving normalized water flux by roughly 15% in mineral scaling tests and 10% in biofouling tests relative to the uncoated membrane—driven mainly by GO's antimicrobial activity and both materials' effect on surface charge and hydrophilicity. The results show that antiscaling materials can work as membrane coatings, not just as feed additives, though pairing them with an antimicrobial coating is what actually resolves biofouling.
