Moving fluids through fractures and pore networks—whether proppant carried by supercritical CO2, or hydrogen and hydrocarbons through shale—depends on flow regimes and transport mechanisms that differ sharply from conventional reservoir models. My work uses computational fluid dynamics and multiscale semi-analytical modeling to characterize this behavior across several systems: proppant transport with supercritical CO2 in single- and multi-fracture geometries, hydrogen storage capacity in partially depleted, hydraulically fractured shale reservoirs, and CO2-enhanced hydrocarbon recovery and sequestration in multiscale shale reservoirs.

The proppant-transport studies identified operating windows—particle density, size, sand ratio, and injection rate—that avoid sand blocking and produce a uniform proppant bed, while the shale-reservoir modeling estimated hydrogen storage capacity (up to roughly 3.2 billion m³ in a representative Appalachian Basin case) and its sensitivity to adsorption, diffusion, and reservoir flow properties.
