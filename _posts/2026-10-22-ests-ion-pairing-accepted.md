"Spectroscopic Insights into Ion Pairing in Sulfate Electrolytes: Implications for Sorption in Aqueous Systems" was accepted on October 22, 2026.

Cation exchange membranes (CEMs) show a long-standing trend: potassium sulfate partitions into the membrane more strongly than sodium or lithium sulfate, a trend that partitioning models based on nominal sulfate charge do not predict. We hypothesized that ion pairs such as NaSO₄⁻ form inside the membrane, where the low-dielectric environment favors lower-charge species. Testing this required measuring ion pairing directly, in water and in a low-dielectric medium. I combined Raman and dielectric spectroscopy to quantify contact, solvent-shared, and solvent-separated ion pairs in lithium, sodium, and potassium sulfate solutions, in water and in water-methanol mixtures. The measurements reconcile literature methods that disagree on pairing strength by nearly an order of magnitude. They also point to a mechanism for potassium: its weaker hydration shell lowers the cost of forming direct pairs with sulfate, and its higher polarizability stabilizes them. Models that treat sulfate as a single fixed-valence species miss this effect, which bears on cation selectivity in ion-exchange membranes for desalination and lithium recovery from brines. The dielectric measurements were developed in collaboration with Prof. Vinh Nguyen at Virginia Tech.
